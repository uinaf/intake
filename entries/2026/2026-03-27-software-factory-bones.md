David Crawshaw argues everyone is building a software factory, but no one has a settled team-and-agent workflow. The shared primitive is plentiful, isolated VMs.

## Key takeaways

- **No single solution**: Pushing one workflow is counterproductive. The moment calls for experimentation and agency to iterate.
- **Compute primitive**: Trivial-to-provision, shareable, secure VMs are the substrate from which agents and automations emerge.
- **Divergent workflows**: Seven people at exe.dev produced nine different workflows, from multi-agent orchestrators to Slack bots.
- **Isolation bones**: Successful workflows isolate, try, share, iterate, and parallelize on VMs. Those patterns become the factory's bones.
- **Human-shaped work**: A thread reply argues PRs and sprints were designed for human throughput. The right agent unit may be a test-passing diff plus evals, not code review.
