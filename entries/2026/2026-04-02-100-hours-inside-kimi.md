A Renwu magazine reporter spent 100 hours inside Moonshot AI (Kimi), interviewing 30+ employees at a young, flat, model-first lab that treats taste as the hiring standard.

## Key takeaways

- **Company snapshot**: About 300 people, average age under 30, roughly $16B valuation at three years; five Tsinghua co-founders and a taste for hiring former founders.
- **No hierarchy**: No departments, titles, OKRs, or KPIs; each co-founder works directly with 40–50 people, and more than half of interviewees had changed roles.
- **DeepSeek shock**: The rival's breakout felt devastating, then forced a narrower model-first bet and a hard hiring question: why join Kimi if DeepSeek also offered.
- **Taste hiring**: Hire generalists who transfer across domains, not people overfit to one company's KPI system; Kimi CLI is cited as ~400 lines meant to feel like a smart shell.
- **Two-dimensional foil**: The firm deliberately flattens hierarchy and department walls so only model and intelligence face each other. This is an irreversible cultural gamble.
- **Agent-native work**: Employees launch parallel agents for daily work; humans set rules, silicon executes, and the organization is described as a container for algorithms.
- **Acknowledged fragility**: Without hierarchy to buffer uncertainty, direction can feel foggy; the flat model may hit limits around 500 people.
