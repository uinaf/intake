# 100 Hours Inside Kimi (Moonshot AI)

Translated by Rui Ma from Renwu (人物) magazine. Reporter spent 100 hours inside Moonshot AI (Kimi), interviewing 30+ employees.

## Company snapshot
- 300 employees, average age under 30, ~80% from China's elite 985/211 universities
- Valuation: RMB 120B (~$16B), 3 years old
- ~50 employees have prior startup/founding experience ("Kimi likes hiring CEOs")
- 80% are self-described introverts

## Organizational model
- **No departments, no titles, no hierarchy, no OKRs, no KPIs**
- Each co-founder interfaces directly with 40-50 people
- All 5 co-founders from Tsinghua
- Communication norm: direct, skip managers, no approval chains
- 50%+ of interviewees had changed roles multiple times within the company
- Conference rooms named after bands (Radiohead, OK Computer)

## "DeepSeek saved us"
- DeepSeek's breakout was initially devastating (made Kimi feel like yesterday's news)
- But internally it was also exciting: proved a Chinese startup could earn global respect with strong models
- Forced strategic realignment: narrow focus, model-first
- HR's brutal question: "When candidates have a DeepSeek offer too, why should they join Kimi?"

## Hiring philosophy: "Taste is all you need"
- Hire for generalization ability, not specialization
- People overfit to one company's KPI system = overfit models that can't generalize
- Want "base models" (transferable across domains) not "specialized models"
- Taste = highest hiring standard, hardest to define, visible everywhere
- Ex: Kimi CLI designed to feel like a smart shell, not a chat window. Core logic: ~400 lines of Python.

## The "two-dimensional foil" thesis
- Reference to Three-Body Problem: Kimi deliberately "flattens" itself
- No vertical hierarchy depth, no horizontal department walls, no 3D office politics
- Only "model" and "intelligence" facing each other directly
- Irreversible gamble: can't add hierarchy back without breaking the culture
- Bet: if model intelligence crosses critical threshold, org structure advantages of competitors become irrelevant

## Agent-native workflows
- Employees launch multiple agents in parallel for daily work (strategy agent, translation agent, competitor agent)
- "Humans set rules, silicon executes, the organization becomes a container for algorithms"
- Using their own model as both the goal and the tool

## Resilience as core trait
- "Smart and brave are sometimes opposites. The smarter you are, the more clearly you see the risks, and the easier it becomes to walk away."
- MoBA long-context: "three trips to the cliff of reflection" — shelved twice, succeeded on third attempt
- Multiple employees cried more at Kimi than at any previous job — and stayed

## Fragility acknowledged
- Without hierarchy to buffer uncertainty, confusion about direction is felt directly
- "Some mornings you walk into the office not knowing what you should do"
- Flat structure may hit limits around 500 people (historical precedent: holacracy, Haier)
- "If Alibaba is a promotion conveyor belt, ByteDance a ruthless battle corps, and Tencent a professional academy, then Moonshot AI is a primeval forest. Geniuses may find a hunting path. Ordinary people may just wander in the fog."
