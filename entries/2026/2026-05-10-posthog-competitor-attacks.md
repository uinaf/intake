# Why Attacking Your Competitors Online Is Dumb

PostHog argues that public competitor attacks rarely work, even when the complaint is basically true.

Key points:

- Public attacks mostly reach your own fans and the competitor's fans; neither group is the actual buyer evaluating options.
- Going on offense invites scrutiny, and the attacker is probably guilty of some version of the same behavior.
- In open source especially, attacking others for being "inspired" by your work can undercut the point of open ecosystems.
- Naming a competitor gives them free distribution to your audience and makes them look important enough to be upset about.
- Teams overestimate how many customers saw the original offense because they monitor the market far more closely than users do.
- Public rebuttals can amplify the original thing more than they correct it.

Better moves:

- Mock trends, not named companies.
- Publish honest comparison pages that clearly state tradeoffs instead of fake all-green scorecards.
- Bid on competitor keywords when useful; capture people already searching in the category.
- Correct factual errors privately when they matter.
- Convert anger into shipping and product quality.

**Take:** Competitor drama feels productive because it scratches the justice itch, but strategically it is usually audience leakage plus emotional labor. The higher-leverage response is boring and brutal: clearer positioning, honest comparisons, private corrections, and outshipping them.
