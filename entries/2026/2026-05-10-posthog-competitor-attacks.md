PostHog argues that public competitor attacks rarely work, even when the complaint is true. They mostly reach fans, invite scrutiny, and give the other side free distribution.

## Key takeaways

- **Wrong audience**: Attacks reach your fans and theirs, not buyers who are actually evaluating options.
- **Free distribution**: Naming a competitor sends them your audience and makes them look important enough to fight.
- **Self-scrutiny**: Going on offense invites the same critique, and open-source "inspired by" attacks can undercut the ecosystem.
- **Amplification**: Teams overestimate how many customers saw the original offense. Public rebuttals can spread it further.
- **Better moves**: Mock trends, publish honest comparison pages, bid on competitor keywords, correct facts privately, and ship.
