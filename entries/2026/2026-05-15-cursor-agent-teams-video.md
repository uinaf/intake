The talk splits software work into three eras: manual editing, autocomplete assistance, and teams of agents. Cursor says agent requests have overtaken tab completions, including inside its own company.

## Key takeaways

- **Three eras**: Manual editing, then autocomplete or single-agent help, then parallel agent teams.
- **Self-reported mix**: Cursor claims about 30% of its PRs are developed end-to-end by remote cloud agents, and enterprise AI-generated code rose from roughly 15–20% to about 75% in a year.
- **Manager role**: Engineers scope, launch many attempts, compare, test, review, and merge rather than typing most syntax.
- **Quality risk**: Weak review and architecture discipline can produce lots of unsustainable code. Generation percentage is a vanity metric without gates.
- **Nascent demo**: A week-long autonomous team built a mostly functional browser prototype. It is presented as experimental, not production-ready.
- **Self-reported numbers**: Interesting internal figures, not a neutral benchmark.
