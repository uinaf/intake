Sean Goedecke argues workplace anger is a career local maximum: it often comes from caring and competence, but it makes colleagues route around you and shrinks your influence.

## Key takeaways

- **Anger as extra work**: Visible anger turns you into a problem to manage. Meetings go quiet, and people stop bringing you issues.
- **Routing around damage**: Teams exclude emotionally unreliable engineers from decisions, which makes them angrier and more isolated.
- **Care overflow**: Anger usually comes from caring. Too little investment produces coasting; too much, especially when misaligned with the company, overflows into blowups.
- **Celebrity exception**: Linus or Cantrill can stay angry because they control the work. Copying talk-and-blog intensity overshoots everyday team work.
- **Performed frustration**: A little visible frustration can signal humanity if you can drop it instantly. Genuine anger fails that test; when unsure, stay professional.
- **Local maximum**: Anger feels like proof you matter. Dropping it often increases output and leaves energy for problems that actually count.
- **Valid exceptions**: Anger at company misconduct is warranted. Habitual anger at problems still leaks onto the people nearby.
