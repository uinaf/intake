Falk and Tsoukalas model AI automation as a demand externality: firms capture the full cost saving but only a slice of the demand they destroy, so over-automation is a dominant strategy.

## Key takeaways

- **Structural trap**: Perfect foresight does not fix it. Each firm's optimal automation rate exceeds the cooperative optimum.
- **Competition worsens it**: A monopolist internalizes the externality; fragmented markets show the widest gap.
- **Red Queen**: Higher AI productivity widens the wedge. Symmetric gains cancel, leaving extra distortion.
- **Wages and transfers**: Endogenous wages change when the problem bites, not whether it exists. Both workers and owners take a deadweight loss.
- **Failed instruments**: Upskilling, worker equity, Coasian bargains, capital taxes, and UBI narrow or cushion the harm but do not hit the per-task incentive.
- **Pigouvian tax**: A tax equal to the uninternalized demand loss per task is the only instrument that implements the cooperative optimum.
- **Not coordination**: Unlike a talk-it-out failure, this is a true externality with a dominant-strategy equilibrium.
